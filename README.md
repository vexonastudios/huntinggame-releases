## New in 1.10.3: faster ducks and fewer easy doubles

Ducks arrive in separated lanes, take off at staggered times and fan out into faster individual paths with different heights and banking turns. Tighter body hit detection and a narrower duck-hunt pellet pattern reward tracking and leading a bird. Genuine doubles still count when pellets reach two birds.

Junior keeps slower flight and modest aim forgiveness. County Roundup ducks share the improved flight and hit detection. Clay courses, other weapon settings and graphics budgets retain their existing rules. Update the host to use the revised duck rules; updating all family computers is recommended.

## New in 1.10.2: wary deer and a smoother gallop

Deer now flee when approached closely from any direction, keep escaping while pursued, steer around trunks and map edges, and spread apart instead of stacking at one cover point. Junior still has gentler awareness, but deer can outrun a sprinting hunter.

Running now uses coordinated hoof contacts, hind-leg push-off, suspension and landing, with forward neck posture and a raised tail. Hooves stay planted during ground contact and legs keep their correct lengths. Nearby-tree queries keep the improved escape behavior inexpensive.

Update all family computers for the revised behavior and animation. Profiles, controls and the LAN protocol are unchanged.

- **Deer hunting:** November woods and meadow, hoofprint trails, rustling clues and a scoped rifle. Adult whitetails feed, listen, walk and bound into cover; movement, noise and wind affect their awareness.

**Family LAN:** everyone needs **1.10.0 or newer** to play together. Older versions use an incompatible wildlife protocol. Profiles and progress are preserved. Press **Esc** for the menu or **F11** to switch to a window.

## New in 1.9.6: richer High landscapes

Missouri hardwoods keep their bare November branches. The creek has softer flowing currents and the marsh gains finer ripples and shoreline highlights. ## New in 1.9.5: graphics for different computers

**High** keeps detailed scenery, shadows, reflections and smooth edges. **Low** now uses cheaper bark, soil and foliage materials, simpler farm and clay scenery, and fewer decorative grass tufts. Concealment bushes, playable branches and all animals remain present. Tree geometry is more efficient on every preset.

**Auto** can step down to a smaller 3D image if a computer remains slow; menus, text and crosshairs stay sharp. Distant animation uses less CPU on Low and Medium, with full posing restored for nearby and magnified wildlife. Manual settings remain your choice. Open **Esc → Settings** to choose Auto, Low, Medium or High; **Recheck this computer** restarts Auto.

Measured rendering workload fell by 57% for squirrel High and 43% for hog Low in fixed-view development-PC samples. These are fewer rendered primitives, not guaranteed frame rates on every computer. Saved hunters, dogs, progress, voices and controls are preserved. Versions 1.9.3 through 1.9.6 share the same LAN protocol.

## Your hunter, dog, camp and County Roundup

**Switch hunter / dog** on the home screen gives each child their own appearance, companion, camp progress and records. Name an adult golden retriever with a golden, cream or red-golden coat. Your buddy follows on roaming hunts, sniffs nearby visible wildlife and retrieves ducks; press **E** nearby to say hello. Dogs stay protected from shots. Fixed tower/bench and clay activities leave dogs at camp.

Choose a custom camp sign and earn a dog bed, achievement board and campfire. **Walk around camp** opens a small area with weapons stored and a clear Esc return path. Existing saves are preserved; profiles stay local to each computer.

**County roundup** combines ducks, squirrels, hogs and prairie dogs on one reserve with one timer and score. Explore the woods, pond, pasture and burrows. Press **1** for the shotgun or **2** for the scoped rifle; each keeps its own ammunition. Junior starts a two-minute co-op outing; Classic supports custom pacing and friendly competition. Deer hunting is planned for a later update.

**Solo Shot Coach** explains accepted shots using actual projectile paths: high/low, left/right, too much or too little lead, intervening cover, or a prairie dog retreating. Its diagram refers to the nearest visible target. Coaching can be disabled in Settings and does not appear in multiplayer. LAN players need compatible builds (**1.9.3 through 1.9.6**).

## Automatic optimization and quicker prairie dogs

Distant animals now use simpler geometry, with full detail restored when you approach or zoom through the scope. Squirrels share drawing work and prairie-dog models are reused between appearances. Auto targets up to 60 FPS; menus and unfocused solo play use less rendering work while active LAN rounds stay responsive. Actual performance still depends on the computer.

Prairie dogs no longer stay up as long: fully standing windows vary around 1½–3 seconds in Junior and 0.8–1.8 seconds in Classic, shortening during the round. Retreats are faster. Earlier slower-course records are preserved separately, so each child can set a new best on this quicker course.

### Graphics for slower computers

The squirrel woods have a lighter **Low** preset: no falling leaves, drifting dust, footstep particles, shadows or reflections. Simpler tree meshes, water and ground details reduce rendering work while preserving the squirrels, playable branches and hunting rules. The 3D image scales down; menus and crosshairs stay sharp.

**Auto** adjusts graphics when play stays slow and remembers the result on each computer. Open **Esc → Settings → Low** for an immediate change, or choose Auto. Medium and High also have a separate falling-leaves switch. Existing saves and manual Low/High choices are preserved.

## Prairie Dog Lookout

Choose **Prairie dog lookout** for a timed challenge at a covered shooting table. A loaded scoped rifle, twenty burrows and two minutes: watch for stirring dirt, then score as prairie dogs pop up and look around. Junior offers longer openings at nearer burrows; Classic is quicker and includes farther targets. The setting is open dry grassland, with original tan prairie dogs, tiny ears and dark tail tips.

Play solo and keep a personal best under each hunter name, or choose **Host 1v1 LAN / Join 1v1 LAN**. The field is shared, every animal scores once, and the higher score at two minutes wins. Results include accuracy and quick replay. Both players should update to **1.9.6**. Hold right mouse to scope, left-click to shoot, R to reload, and wheel / Z to zoom.

**Hog Tower Duel** also remains available: two fixed towers, a shared field, first to 10 hogs or the higher score after three minutes. The original roaming hunts, climbable farm lookout and clay range remain available.
